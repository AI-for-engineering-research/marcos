**Research Project**
*What?* The formation of contrails depends on how ice particles form in young exhaust. Persistence and climate forcing of contrails is dependent on combined effects of ice particle distribution and ambient conditions. Therefore, understanding ice particle formation is a key aspect of contrail impact assessments. We are interested in modeling the behavior of contrail formation and persistence for an array of engine design parameters. Low order Gaussian plume approximation models do not capture the relevant early physics, whereas higher order LES (large eddy simulations) are too computationally expensive for our purposes. An appropriate starting point is the intermediate fidelity model APCEMM. While successful in modeling some important microphysics in the early plume stages, APCEMM provides a limited view of how exhaust conditions affect ice particle activation. In particular, the role of sulfur and it's competition for water content and ice formation contributions are not captured. The challenge in this research is the limited understanding of the effect of different engine exhaust parameters on ice particle activation. Improving our contrail model considering soot and FSC sensitivities will provide insight on the potential impacts of using sustainable aviation fuels on contrail formation. 

*How?* Approach: Develop advanced early plume model considering the role of FSC and soot EI on ice particle activation and growth, and incorporate into the current APCEMM framework. Couple new model with rapid radiative transfer models to estimate the sensitivity of climate forcing to varying engine design parameters and representative ambient conditions.Overall goal: Capture sensitivities of contrail lifetime and effective forcing to changes in FSC, plume exhaust parameters, and ambient conditions.

*What? Hypthesized findings* Quantification of the effect of FSC and soot on contrail formation and average RF sensitivities from a representative physics based approach. Assessment of the Schmidt-Appleman criteria, will sulfur play a role in this established criteria?

**Heilheim Questions** What are you trying to do?  (Articulate your objectives using absolutely no jargon)
	• Develop an early plume physics model that considers the effect of fuel sulfur content and soot EI on ice particle activation and contrail formation.
	• Explore the sensitivity of engine parameters on contrail formation behavior.
	• I.e, change in sulfur leads to how much change in RF?

How is it done today and what are the limits of current practice?
	• Contrail models include:
		○ Low order, Gaussian plume approximations that, while computationally efficient, does not consider relevant microphysics
		○ Intermediate order models (APCEMM) that consider some relevant microphysics, are also more time efficient than LES, but require additional physics for our purposes.
		○ Large eddy simulations (LES) solves almost all of the physics regarding contrail evolution, but too computationally expensive.

What is new in your approach and why do you think it will be successful?
	• We will connect contrail RF sensitivities to engine exhaust parameters.
	• Including the role of FSC on ice particle activation in our model will have a direct effect on contrail formation and persistence. 

Who cares?  If it is successful, what difference will it make?
	• Success would mean a connection between the engine design parameters and contrail formation.
	• Coupling the advanced microphysics model with a radiative transfer model would provide a link between engine design and contrail climate forcing impact.
Results would lead to the assessment of the validity of the SA criteria for modern turbofan engines.
